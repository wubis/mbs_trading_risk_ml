PART 2: MODELING LOSS

Modeling setup

I treated the task as current-state severity estimation because predictions.csv includes updated occupancy, updated NOI, and delinquency flags. Those fields would be leakage for an origination-time forecast, so part2.ipynb also fits an origination-only sensitivity model. Because the assignment defines severity on the interval from zero to one, the primary target clips the 108 supplied values above one; the raw supplied target is retained as a separately labeled linear-model sensitivity.

I excluded loan_id and propname, joined property attributes with a validated many-to-one merge, and used an 80/20 GroupShuffleSplit on propname to prevent loans from the same property crossing the train-validation boundary. The split contains 31,790 training loans and 7,909 validation loans, with positive-severity rates of 16.16% and 15.72%, respectively.

Features used in the required linear regression

1. log_loan_size: Loan balance measures exposure size, and log1p limits the influence of unusually large loans.
2. ltv_fraction: Higher leverage leaves less collateral cushion after default.
3. occ_at_orig_fraction: Origination occupancy describes the property's initial demand and underwriting condition.
4. signed_log_noi_orig: Origination NOI measures initial debt-service capacity while the signed log reduces skew.
5. loan_term_years: Contractual term may affect maturity and refinancing risk.
6. origination_year: Vintage can proxy for underwriting standards and market regimes.
7. property_age: Older properties may face greater physical or functional obsolescence.
8. property_age_invalid: This flag identifies the 848 records with a negative implied property age before those ages are imputed.
9. log_sqft: Property size may affect liquidity and recovery, and log1p reduces its heavy right skew.
10. occ_fraction: Current occupancy measures the property's latest operating condition.
11. occupancy_change: The change from origination captures operating improvement or deterioration.
12. signed_log_noi_current: Current NOI measures the latest income supporting the debt while retaining negative values.
13. noi_relative_change: Relative NOI change captures operating deterioration using a stable denominator and fixed clipping.
14. is_30d: Thirty-day delinquency is a direct indicator of current credit stress.
15. is_90d: Ninety-day delinquency identifies more advanced payment distress.
16. cssaproptype: Broad property type captures sector differences without relying on inconsistent detailed labels.
17. msa_category: MSA category provides a coarse measure of market and location.
18. state: State captures additional geographic variation with protection for unseen categories.
19. office_group: The Part 1 grouping distinguishes dedicated office, any other property whose detailed type contains office, and non-office exposure.

Numeric features use training-fold median imputation, missingness indicators, and standardization; categorical features use normalized text, most-frequent imputation, and one-hot encoding with unseen-category handling. Dates are converted into loan term and origination year, while the high-cardinality proptypelong field is excluded.

Linear-regression results

On the property-grouped holdout, linear regression produced R-squared of 0.525208 and MSE of 0.017876 against the bounded target. The scatter plot shows predictions concentrated around a few average levels rather than the 45-degree line; predictions range from -0.1854 to 0.6732, with 31.12% below zero.

Why does ordinary linear regression perform poorly?

Severity has a large point mass at zero and a wide, right-skewed positive distribution even after enforcing the stated upper bound. Delinquency, leverage, occupancy decline, and NOI decline are also likely to have nonlinear and interacting effects. Squared error emphasizes relatively large losses, while the raw supplied target also contains 108 values outside the stated range. Finally, an unbounded linear model produces economically invalid negative severities.

Recommended alternative

I tested a two-stage hurdle model that multiplies the predicted probability of positive severity by a conditional-loss estimate from gradient-boosted trees and constrains the result to the valid zero-to-one range. On the same holdout it improved R-squared to 0.595655, reduced MSE to 0.015224 and MAE to 0.047678, and eliminated out-of-range predictions. Across 15 paired property-grouped folds, it reduced mean MSE by 15.93% and mean MAE by 26.81%, outperforming linear regression in every fold.

Caveats and scoring

The current-state fields provide most of the signal: the origination-only linear model reached R-squared of 0.069687 and MSE of 0.035026. Current occupancy, NOI, and delinquency should therefore be used only when available at the intended prediction date. A raw-target linear sensitivity reached R-squared of 0.517905 and MSE of 0.018753, so enforcing the stated target range does not change the qualitative conclusion.

Because the bounded hurdle model has the lowest mean grouped-CV MSE, part2.ipynb refits it on all labeled loans and writes 1,000 nonmissing predictions between 0.0001 and 0.7939 to outputs/predictions_scored.csv without modifying predictions.csv.
